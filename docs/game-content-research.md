## §1 — Materials & Fabrics

### Current options in game
`cotton` `fleece` `technical` `denim` `leather`

### Recommended additions
- **Waffle knit** — highest priority; distinctive texture, growing streetwear adoption, premium positioning
- **Rib knit** — enables fitted/structured silhouettes currently absent from the list
- **French terry** — sits between cotton and fleece; breathable, spring/fall use, popular with premium basics brands

---

### Cotton

**Variants**
| Variant | GSM | Feel | Best for |
|---------|-----|------|----------|
| Standard jersey | 150–180 | Light, breathable, slight drape | Summer tees, layering |
| Heavyweight jersey | 260–300 | Structured, body | Streetwear tees, oversized |
| Combed cotton | 180–280 | Smooth, anti-pill | Premium basics |
| Ring-spun | 180–280 | Ultra-soft, expensive | Luxury basics |

**Premium signals**: Combed or ring-spun. Shrinkage <3%. No pilling after 20 washes.  
**Budget signals**: Visible pilling within 5 washes. Color bleeds. Shrinkage 5–8%.

**Brand examples**: Reigning Champ (heavyweight), Sunspel (ring-spun), Uniqlo (combed at scale)

**Missing from game that matters**: Waffle knit (see below), rib knit

---

### Fleece

**Variants**
| Variant | GSM | Feel | Best for |
|---------|-----|------|----------|
| Standard brushed fleece | 280–320 | Plush, warm, heavy drape | Hoodies, crewnecks |
| Heavyweight fleece | 400–500 | Very thick, premium feel | Premium streetwear |
| French terry | 280–350 | Smooth exterior, looped interior, breathable | Spring/fall hoodies |
| Anti-pill fleece | 300–400 | Maintains softness 50+ washes | Long-term basics |

**Premium signals**: 400+ GSM. Anti-pill finish. Combed yarn. Consistent dye batch.  
**Budget signals**: Matted appearance after one season. <280 GSM feels thin.

**Brand examples**: Reigning Champ (430+ GSM), Stüssy (430 GSM, 75/25 cotton-poly), Fear of God Essentials (premium soft fleece)

**Key spec to know**: Cotton-poly blend ratio. 100% polyester = cheapest warmest. 60% cotton / 40% poly = premium feel. 80% cotton / 20% poly = luxury.

---

### Technical

**Variants**
| Variant | GSM | Properties | Best for |
|---------|-----|------------|----------|
| Polyester moisture-wicking | 200–250 | Fast-drying, wrinkle-resistant | Athletic hoodies, joggers |
| Nylon blend | 200–300 | Durable, wind/water resistant | Performance outerwear |
| 4-way stretch (polyester/spandex) | 200–250 | Movement, recovery | Fitted athletic cuts |
| Recycled polyester | 200–280 | Same performance, sustainability story | Eco brand positioning |

**Premium signals**: Matte finish. Invisible wicking treatment. Certified (Polartec, DWR). Flatlock seams.  
**Budget signals**: Plastic-y feel. Color fades in <10 washes. Stiff wicking coating.

**Brand examples**: Nike Tech Fleece, Patagonia (recycled poly), Arc'teryx (nylon-poly)

---

### Denim

**Variants**
| Variant | Weight | Best for |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Lightweight shirting denim | 5–6 oz (150–180 GSM) | Shirts, oversized tees |
| Mid-weight denim | 8–10 oz (240–280 GSM) | Jackets, structured shirts |
| Heavyweight / selvedge | 12–14 oz (340–400 GSM) | Heritage jackets, premium positioning |
| Stretch denim | +2–3% elastane | Fitted silhouettes |

**Premium signals**: Selvedge edge (narrow loom). Even indigo fade. Japanese mill origin.  
**Budget signals**: Stiff when new and never softens. Blotchy fading.

**Brand examples**: Levi's 501 Selvedge, Pure Blue Japan, Uniqlo denim (volume mid-weight)

**Production note**: Denim is hard for small brands — MOQ typically 1,000+ kg. Consider as an aspirational material or "unlock" in later gameplay.

---

### Leather (and alternatives)

| Type | Cost | Durability | Story |
|------|------|------------|-------|
| Full-grain leather | $$$ | Excellent, develops patina | Heritage, luxury |
| Top-grain leather | $$ | Very good | Premium, consistent |
| PU leather (vegan) | $ | Fair–good | Modern, accessible |
| Cactus leather (Piñatex) | $$ | Good | Sustainable premium |
| Waxed canvas | $ | Excellent | Workwear/utility heritage |

**Brand examples**: APC (full-grain), Deadwood (cactus leather), Carhartt WIP (waxed canvas)

---

### Waffle Knit _(recommended addition)_

Textured knit with air pockets (diamond pattern). Warm without bulk. Vintage/heritage aesthetic. Growing strongly in 2025–26 streetwear (Aritzia, Public Rec, Barefoot Dreams).

- GSM: 180–280
- Feel: Textured, warm, breathable
- Brands: Aritzia, Public Rec

---

### French Terry _(recommended addition)_

Smooth exterior with uncut loops on interior. Breathes well, hangs with intentional drape. Not as heavy as fleece.

- GSM: 280–380
- Feel: Balanced — not as warm as fleece, more structure than jersey
- Brands: Reigning Champ, Aimé Leon Dore, Tasc Performance
- Garment-dyed options create distinctive faded aesthetic

## §2 — Silhouettes & Garment Construction

### Current options in game (both garments)
`cropped` `regular` `oversized` `boxy`

### Recommended additions for t-shirts
- **Relaxed fit** — fills the gap between regular and oversized; important for comfort-first brands
- **Longline** — length-focused, reads editorial, trend-forward
- **Muscle fit** — niche but growing; adds body-type specificity

---

### Hoodie Silhouettes

| Silhouette | Chest ease | Shoulder drop | Body length | Construction complexity | Fabric consumption | Aesthetic |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|-------------|------------------------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Regular** | +2–3% | At shoulder | 27–29" | Low | 1.8–2.1 m | Heritage, classic athletic |
| **Oversized** | +15–25% | 2–4" drop | 29–32" | High | 2.4–2.8 m | Contemporary streetwear, hip-hop |
| **Cropped** | +2–5% | At shoulder | 16–20" | Low | 1.5–1.7 m | Y2K, Gen Z, festival |
| **Boxy** | +18–25% | 1–2" drop | 18–22" | Moderate-high | 2.0–2.3 m | Japanese minimalism, luxury casual |

**MOQ implications**: Regular/Cropped = 100–150 units. Oversized/Boxy = 150–300 units (more complex grading).

**What makes oversized different from regular (for tooltips)**: Shoulder seam is repositioned 2–4 inches past the natural shoulder — it's not just "bigger", the whole pattern is re-drafted. Fabric consumption is 30–40% more.

**Brand associations**:
- Regular: Champion Reverse Weave, Carhartt WIP, Patagonia
- Oversized: Stüssy, Carhartt WIP Oversized, Represent Initial
- Cropped: Los Angeles Apparel, coordinated sets brands
- Boxy: Auralee, WELLDER, Represent Initial Boxy

## §3 — Details, Hardware & Trims

### Hoodie details

#### Hood construction
| Option | Description | Signal |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| 2-panel | Single center seam, pointed shape | Budget |
| 3-panel _(standard)_ | Center gusset, rounder shape, better fit | Standard |
| Panelled (4+) | Multiple panels, structured | Premium |

**Lining**: Unlined (light/budget) → Single-lined jersey → Double-lined (premium, maximum warmth)  
**Logo tab on hood seam**: Small detail, big signal. Supreme, Stüssy use this.

#### Pockets
| Type | Description | Signal |
|------|-------------|--------|
| Kangaroo | Single pouch, both-side entry | Standard |
| Split kangaroo | Center divider creating two pouches | Mid-range |
| Welt | Flat inset pocket with piping | Premium/technical |
| Chest pocket | Single small chest patch | Utility/workwear |

**Quality signals**: Bartacks at pocket corners (prevents tearing). Bound top opening. Clean stitching.

#### Zippers
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Full zip | Hem to neck, maximum versatility |
| Half zip | To mid-chest, sporty |
| Quarter zip | To upper chest, athletic |

**YKK vs generic**: YKK zippers glide smoothly, rarely separate, last years. Generic zipper pulls can feel gritty; failure rate is meaningfully higher. Cost difference: +$0.40–0.70/unit. For a premium brand, always YKK.

**Pull material**: Metal pulls (brass/zinc) = premium. Paint-dipped plastic = budget (paint chips).

**Concealed zipper**: Hidden behind placket. Premium construction signal.

#### Drawstrings
| Element | Budget | Premium |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Cord | Round twisted cord | Flat woven cord (6–15mm) |
| Aglets | Rubber/plastic | Metal (brass, zinc, anodized) |
| Eyelets | Plastic holes | Metal eyelets sewn in |

**Flat cord + metal aglets** is one of the clearest budget vs. premium visual signals players can see.

#### Ribbing
| Type | Description | Signal |
|------|-------------|--------|
| 1x1 rib | Fine, minimal texture | Lightweight, budget |
| 2x1 rib | Medium ridges | Standard quality |
| 2x2 rib | Prominent ridges, thick | Premium, holds shape |

**2x2 rib on a winter hoodie = premium signal.** After many washes, 2x2 rib maintains its structure where cheaper options flatten.

#### Premium hoodie details (add these as toggle options)
- **Split hem** — side vents for movement
- **Gussets** — triangular inserts at underarm stress points, extends lifespan
- **Thumbholes** — extended cuff with thumb opening
- **Flatlock seams** — flat construction, no bulk, no chafing (athletic/premium standard)
- **Double-stitched stress points** — bartacks at pocket corners, zipper top

| Neckline | Aesthetic | Premium signals |
|----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Crew (standard) | Universal, classic | Even rib, no puckering |
| V-neck (shallow) | Professional, layering | Reinforced V seam |
| V-neck (deep) | Fashion-forward | Same |

|------|----------|-----------|

| Cap | Minimal coverage | Feminine, warm weather |

#### Sleeve length
`cap` → `short` → `3/4` → `long`

**3/4 sleeve** is worth adding — contemporary, growing popularity in 2026.

#### Hem options
| Hem | Description | Best for |
|-----|-------------|----------|
| Straight | Clean, universal | Casual, untucked |
| Curved | Follows body, tailored | Fitted, tucked |
| Drop tail | Back longer than front | Oversized, layering |
| Shirt-tail | Shaped, designed for tucking | Heritage, smart-casual |
| Side slits/vents | Ventilation + movement | Athletic, contemporary |

**Finishing quality**: Double-stitched > single-stitched. Blind/coverstitch hem = premium.

#### Graphic placements
| Placement | Size | Aesthetic |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| Left chest | 3–4" | Premium, understated |
| Center chest | 8" | Classic streetwear |
| Full front | 10–12" | Graphic-forward, statement |
| Back upper | 6–8" | Secondary brand statement |
| Full back | 12–14" | Event merch, bold brands |
| Sleeve | 2–4" | Contemporary, layered looks |

**2026 trend**: Oversized front graphics (11–12") dominant. Back prints growing.

## §4 — Brand Identity Archetypes

### Current options in game
`minimal` `bold` `streetwear` `luxury` `utility`

### Recommended additions
- **Heritage/Craft** — process and maker story over price; Comme des Garçons, Kapital, Visvim
- **Contemporary/Conceptual** — designer as brand; Jacquemus, A-COLD-WALL, Doublet

---

### Minimal

**Philosophy**: Beauty through restraint. Subtraction as confidence. Timelessness over trends.

**Real brands**: The Row, Jil Sander, Acne Studios, COS, Everlane

**Typography**: Light geometric sans (Helvetica, Futura Light). Generous letter-spacing. Never heavy or condensed.

**Palette**: Black, white, cream, grey, soft taupe. Max 3–4 colours. One deliberate accent if any.

**Tone examples**:
- _"Essential. Timeless. Built to last."_
- _"Design without distraction."_
- _"Considered simplicity."_

**Typical customer**: Architecture-minded creatives, 30+. See clothing as wardrobe infrastructure.

**Garment signals**: Merino, Italian cotton, tonal stitching, invisible zippers, no logos, precise silhouettes.

**Try-hard signal**: Blank beige basics with no point of view. Boring, not restrained.

---

### Bold

**Philosophy**: Fashion as provocation. Indifference is the crime.

**Real brands**: Balenciaga (Demna era), Vivienne Westwood, GCDS, Diesel

**Typography**: Heavy geometric sans (Futura Bold), compressed, all-caps. Tight kerning. Type as image.

**Palette**: High saturation + high contrast. Neons, electric tones. 4–6+ colours. Colour clashing is intentional.

**Tone examples**:
- _"Break the rules. Break yourself. Rebuild."_
- _"Excessive. Uncompromising. Unapologetic."_
- _"Fashion is a weapon. Use it."_

**Typical customer**: Artists, musicians, creatives 18–35. Fashion as identity and language.

**Garment signals**: Exaggerated proportions, large-scale graphic prints, hardware as design (not function), asymmetry, cut-outs.

**Try-hard signal**: Loud for the sake of loud. No cohesion. Feels desperate, not ideological.

---

### Streetwear

**Philosophy**: Community, authenticity, cultural authority. Real fashion comes from the street.

**Real brands**: Supreme, Stüssy, Carhartt WIP, Stray Rats

**Typography**: Bold sans (Futura, Franklin Gothic), all-caps or mixed case. Oblique/italic common. Graffiti-adjacent. Large on chest/back.

**Palette**: Black, white, grey as base. One bold accent (red, forest green, navy, mustard). Colour often references subculture codes.

**Tone examples**:
- _"Built for the culture. Recognized by the culture."_
- _"Not for everyone. For the ones who know."_
- _"Keep it real. Keep it moving."_

**Typical customer**: Skaters, musicians, gamers 18–35. Authenticity is non-negotiable. Selling out is the cardinal sin.

**Garment signals**: Oversized, dropped shoulders, box logos, embroidered patches, branded hardware, limited drops.

**Try-hard signal**: Fast fashion copying drop culture without credibility. No real community. Brand shouting "streetwear" instead of being it.

---

### Luxury

**Philosophy**: Heritage, craftsmanship, exclusivity. Clothing as investment and cultural capital.

**Two modes**: Loud luxury (visible logos, confidence in price) vs. Quiet luxury ("if you know, you know").

**Real brands**: Hermès, Chanel, Louis Vuitton (loud), The Row, Loro Piana (quiet)

**Typography**: Serif (Didot, Garamond) or refined sans. Light weight. Generous letter-spacing. Centered. Feels like it could be engraved.

**Palette**: Classic, historically rooted. Black, ivory, gold, jewel tones. Leather colours (burgundy, tan, caramel). 2–4 colours per collection.

**Tone examples**:
- _"Since [year]. Still made the same way."_
- _"Impeccable in all things."_
- _"The kind of beauty that lasts."_

**Typical customer**: 35+, educated, international. Values quality over quantity. Aspiration is the door; heritage is the lock.

**Garment signals**: Rare fabrics (mulberry silk, virgin cashmere), hand-finishing only the wearer sees, custom hardware, monograms, numbered editions, packaging as experience.

**Try-hard signal**: New brands calling themselves "luxury" on price alone. No craft, no heritage. Trend-chasing in a heritage costume.

---

### Utility

**Philosophy**: Beauty from purpose. Form follows function. Earned aesthetic, not designed.

**Real brands**: Carhartt (original), Dickies, Stan Ray, Engineered Garments, Filson

**Typography**: Heavy, utilitarian sans (Franklin Gothic, Akzidenz Grotesk). All-caps. Business-like. Kerning practical.

**Palette**: Khaki, tan, charcoal, black, navy, OD green. Colours earned through material choices. Denim blues, canvas fades, leather patina.

**Tone examples**:
- _"Built for work. Adopted for life."_
- _"Made to last. Wear it in."_
- _"Function first. Everything earned."_

**Typical customer**: Workers, outdoor enthusiasts, skaters, heritage-conscious 25–55. Will wear a garment for 10 years. Respects durability over trends.

**Garment signals**: Canvas, duck cloth, denim. Rivets at stress points. Cargo pockets that fit your hand. Visible seams. Patina is expected.

**Try-hard signal**: Fashion brands in workwear aesthetic without the craft. Fake distressing. Pockets that don't function.

---

### Identity → UI mapping

| Identity | Typography | Palette | Tone | UI character |
|----------|-----------|---------|------|--------------|
| Minimal | Light sans, generous spacing | Black/white/cream, one accent | Quiet, declarative | White space, high contrast, restrained |
| Bold | Heavy sans, tight, all-caps | 4–6 saturated/neon colours | Provocative, emphatic | Dense, colour-forward, graphic |
| Streetwear | Bold sans, oblique, large | Black/white + one bold accent | Insider, "for the culture" | Logo-forward, street-credible |
| Luxury | Serif or refined sans, light | Classic (black, ivory, gold) | Authoritative, heritage | Elegant, spacious, precious |
| Utility | Heavy sans, all-caps, practical | Earth tones, material-inspired | Honest, functional | Workmanlike, hardware-visible |

## §5 — Canton Fair Supplier Landscape

> **Canton Fair Phase 3 (apparel focus): May 1–5, 2026 — 5 days away.**

### Fair structure

- **Area A** — Finished goods, CMT factories, accessories/trims
- **Area C** — Raw materials: fabric mills, yarn suppliers

**Player's design flows**: Fabric choice → Area C mill → Area A CMT factory → Area A trim supplier

---

### Supplier types

#### Fabric mills (Area C)
| Fabric | Typical MOQ | Lead time | Cost/kg |
|--------|------------|-----------|---------|
| Cotton knit (jersey) | 300–500 kg | 4–6 weeks | $2–6 |
| Fleece (cotton-poly) | 300–500 kg | 4–8 weeks | $3–8 |
| Denim | 1,000+ kg | 6–8 weeks | $6–12 |
| Technical/performance | 300–800 kg | 4–6 weeks | $4–10 |

#### CMT factories (Area A) — cut, make, trim

| Complexity | Garment type | MOQ | Lead time |
|-----------|--------------|-----|-----------|
| Low | Basic tee | 100–200 units | 3–4 weeks |
| Medium | Hoodie (hood + drawstring + pocket) | 200–300 units | 4–5 weeks |
| High | Jacket (lining + zipper + hardware) | 300–500 units | 6–8 weeks |

**MOQ reality**: Listed MOQs are high to filter small buyers. Actual negotiable minimum is often 150–300 units for trial orders. Accept 10–20% price premium and ~40–60% of factories will negotiate.

**How to spot a good CMT factory**: Modern Japanese sewing machines. Seam-strength tester on site. BSCI/SEDEX certification. Can show past work portfolio for similar garments.

**70% of Canton Fair exhibitors are trading companies, not factories.** Ask directly: "Do you own your factory?"

#### Trim & accessory suppliers (Area A)

| Item | MOQ | Lead time |
|------|-----|-----------|
| Zippers (YKK equivalent) | 1,000–5,000 units | 2–4 weeks |
| Woven labels | 500–1,000 units | 4–6 weeks |
| Drawstrings + aglets | 2,000–10,000 pairs | 2–3 weeks |
| Metal hardware (buttons, rivets) | 500–2,000 units | 2–3 weeks |
| Embroidered patches | 100–500 units | 3–4 weeks |

---

### Design → supplier mapping

This is the core game mechanic: each design choice routes to a specific supplier type.

| Player choice | Routes to | Difficulty |
|---------------|-----------|------------|
| Cotton jersey, 180 GSM | Cotton knit mill | Easy — many suppliers, low MOQ |
| Heavyweight combed cotton | Premium cotton mill | Medium — fewer suppliers |
| Fleece hoodie | Fleece knit mill | Medium |
| Denim | Denim specialist mill | Hard — 1,000 kg+ MOQ |
| Technical/performance | Technical mill | Medium |
| Basic tee construction | Entry-level CMT | Easy — 100–200 unit MOQ |
| Hoodie with drawstring + pocket | Mid-tier CMT | Medium — 200–300 units |
| Jacket with lining + zipper | Specialist CMT | Hard — 300–500 units |
| Embroidery | Embroidery specialist | Easy — low MOQ (50 units) |
| YKK zipper | Hardware supplier | MOQ 1,000+; justify by brand positioning |
| Woven labels | Label mill | 4–6 week lead time; plan ahead |

### Game data model — supplier lead

When a player completes their design, capture this to generate a supplier match:

```json
{
  "garment_type": "hoodie",
  "fabric": {
    "fiber": "60% cotton / 40% polyester",
    "construction": "knit_fleece",
    "gsm": 300,
    "properties": ["warm", "durable"]
  },
  "construction": {
    "complexity": "medium",
    "features": ["kangaroo_pocket", "drawstring", "hood_3panel"]
  },
  "details": {
    "zipper": "ykk_full_zip",
    "drawstring": "flat_cord_metal_aglets",
    "branding": [
      {"method": "embroidery", "placement": "chest_left"},
      {"method": "woven_label", "placement": "neck"}
    ]
  },
  "quality_tier": "premium",
  "certifications": ["BSCI"],
  "volume": 250,
  "destination": "United Kingdom"
}
```

**Engine use**: Fabric object → filter mills by construction + GSM. Construction → filter CMT by complexity. Details → match trim suppliers + lead times. Quality tier + certs → restrict to vetted suppliers. Destination → route through appropriate 3PL.

### Canton Fair team brief (May 1–5, 2026)

**Objectives**:
1. Recruit 15–25 suppliers interested in game partnership
2. Photograph booths and sample fabrics for game assets
3. Understand real MOQ/pricing firsthand
4. Build relationships before outreach

**Day 1 — Area C (fabrics)**
- Visit 5–8 pre-scheduled mills
- Collect: cotton, fleece, technical fabric swatches
- Ask: MOQ, lead time, certifications, pricing model
- Pitch: "We want to feature your fabrics in a design game. Players can request real quotes."

**Day 2 — Area A (CMT factories)**
- Talk to factory owner or export manager (not sales rep)
- Key questions:
  - "What's your actual minimum for a trial order?"
  - "How do you handle new brands?"
  - "Would you receive pre-qualified design leads from a game platform?"
- Collect past work samples, capability list, certifications

**Day 3 — Area A (trims & accessories)**
- Zippers, labels, drawstrings, hardware
- Short meetings (5–10 min)
- Focus: pricing for 200-unit batches

**Day 4 — deepen relationships**
- Return to top 5–7 prospects
- Show game demo on tablet
- Propose formal partnership

**Day 5 — logistics + debrief**
- Visit 3PL/consolidators if time
- Capture all notes, organise cards

**What to bring**: Business cards with QR code to game. Tablet with demo. Phone power banks.

**Pre-fair (do now, before May 1)**:
1. Download Canton Fair exhibitor directory from cief.cantonfair.org.cn
2. Filter: Textiles & Fabrics (Area C) + Men's/Women's Clothing (Area A) + Accessories (Area A)
3. Shortlist 30 suppliers across mills, CMT, trims
4. Send outreach emails this week: "New fashion brand game platform launching. Interested in discussing partnerships at the fair."

**Post-fair (week after)**:
