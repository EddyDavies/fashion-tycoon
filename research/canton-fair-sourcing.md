# Canton Fair Supplier Landscape & Fashion Sourcing Reference

A comprehensive reference for a web app connecting fashion brand founders to Canton Fair manufacturers. All data verified against 2025–2026 sources.

---

## 1. Canton Fair Overview

### What It Is

The China Import and Export Fair — universally known as the **Canton Fair** — is the world's oldest and largest trade fair, first held in 1957. It runs twice yearly from the China Import and Export Fair Complex (Pazhou Island, Guangzhou, Guangdong Province) and is the single largest sourcing event on the planet by exhibitor count, floor area, and international buyer attendance.

**Official website:** cantonfair.org.cn  
**Venue:** No. 382 Yuejiang Middle Road, Haizhu District, Guangzhou — accessible via Metro Line 8 (Pazhou station)

### 2026 Edition (139th Session) — Schedule

The fair runs in two annual sessions (Spring and Autumn). Each session is divided into three phases, with three-day changeover breaks between phases when exhibitors swap out.

#### Spring 2026

| Phase | Dates | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | April 15–19, 2026 | Electronics, home appliances, auto parts, lighting, hardware, machinery |
| — Break — | April 20–22 | Booth changeover |
| Phase 2 | April 23–27, 2026 | Home goods, gifts, ceramics, furniture, building materials, gardening |
| — Break — | April 28–30 | Booth changeover |
| Phase 3 | May 1–5, 2026 | **Textiles, clothing, footwear, bags, food, toys, medical supplies** |

#### Autumn 2026

| Phase | Dates |
|---|---|
| Phase 1 | October 15–19, 2026 |
| Phase 2 | October 23–27, 2026 |
| Phase 3 | October 31–November 4, 2026 |

Opening hours each day: approximately 09:30–18:00.

### Scale (2025 Data)

The 138th Canton Fair (Autumn 2025) set all-time records:

- **Exhibitors:** Over 32,000 onsite exhibitors (record high)
- **Booths:** 74,600+ (record high)
- **International buyers:** Over 310,000 from 223 countries and regions (7.5% year-on-year increase)
- **Online participation:** 520,000+ buyers via the Canton Fair online platform
- **Export deal value:** ~$25.7 billion in intended export deals
- **Exhibition area:** 1.55 million square metres across the Pazhou Complex

The 137th Canton Fair (Spring 2025) drew 288,938 overseas buyers from 219 countries, with intended export deals of $25.44 billion — up 3% from the previous spring session.

### Organisation by Phase and Hall

The complex is divided into zones (Area A, Area B, Area C) and numbered halls. Phase 3, which is the primary target for fashion buyers, occupies:

- **Area A (Halls 10–13 range):** Men's and Women's Clothing, Kids' Wear, Sports and Casual Wear, Underwear, Shoes, Furs/Leather/Downs, Carpets and Tapestries, Fashion Accessories and Fittings, Traditional Chinese Specialties
- **Area B:** Personal Care Products, Sports/Travel/Recreation, Health Products and Medical Devices, Food, Office Supplies, International Pavilion
- **Area C (Halls 14.1–16.3):** Textiles, Garments, Home Textiles, Medical Devices, Food

The official interactive hall map is at: cantonfair.org.cn/en-US/customPages/tradeAssistant#/hallMap/first. The Canton Fair mobile app provides real-time exhibitor search and booth navigation.

### Phase 2 — What It Actually Covers (Important Clarification)

Phase 2 is often misunderstood as covering clothing. It does **not**. Phase 2 covers:

- Festival products and home decorations
- Gardening products
- Art ceramics and glass artware
- Building and decorative materials
- Gifts and premiums
- Sanitary and bathroom equipment
- Kitchenware and tableware
- Household items
- Clocks, watches, and optical instruments
- Weaving, rattan, and iron products
- Furniture

**For clothing, textiles, and fashion accessories, the correct phase is Phase 3.** In the 2024–2025 editions, nearly 50% of Phase 3 exhibitors specialised in textiles and apparel — it is the dominant category.

## 2. Supplier Types at Canton Fair

### Trading Companies vs. Factories

Approximately 30–40% of Canton Fair exhibitors are trading companies or hybrid operations, despite most presenting themselves as direct manufacturers. This is an industry-wide pattern.

| Dimension | Factory (Direct Manufacturer) | Trading Company |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing | Lower — no intermediary margin | Higher — markup added |
| Product range | Narrower, specialised | Wider — can consolidate multiple SKUs |
| MOQ | Higher — factory setups require volume | Often lower, more negotiable |
| Customisation | Direct OEM discussions | Must relay specs through the factory |
| Communication | Faster — you talk to decision-makers | Slower — filtered through middlemen |
| Quality control | Direct access to production floor | Depends on their factory relationship |
| First-order risk | Lower (assuming verified factory) | Slightly higher if factory relationship is opaque |

**When a trading company makes sense:** You need a mixed-category shipment (multiple product types in one container), your order volume is too small for a factory's MOQ, or you are new to China sourcing and want someone to handle logistics complexity.

**How to identify a factory vs. trading company at the fair:**
- Ask for their business licence (shows registered business scope)
- Ask for factory photos, video tours, and production capacity data
- Ask which other brands they produce for and whether you can visit the facility
- Cross-reference their Alibaba Gold Supplier or SGS verification profile
- A factory's sales staff will typically be able to answer technical production questions (fabric gsm, stitch count, lead time by machine capacity) without consulting anyone; a trader will often say "let me check with our production team"

### Tier-1 Manufacturers: Full-Package vs. CMT

**Full-Package (FP) manufacturers** supply everything: they source fabric, trims, labels, and packaging, and deliver finished garments. You provide tech packs or design references and they manage the entire supply chain. Higher per-unit cost but dramatically lower management overhead. Best for brands without a dedicated sourcing team.

**Cut, Make, Trim (CMT) manufacturers** receive buyer-supplied fabric and trims and charge only for the cut and sew process. Gives the brand complete control over material sourcing. Lower per-unit cost but requires the brand to manage fabric procurement, quality, and logistics separately. Best for brands with established fabric relationships.

### Specialist Suppliers

Canton Fair Phase 3 also hosts specialist upstream suppliers who are not garment manufacturers:

- **Trimmings and notions:** Zippers (YKK alternatives), buttons, rivets, drawcords, elastic, thread
- **Woven and printed labels:** Care labels, brand labels, hangtags, size labels — many suppliers at the fair offer full-service label production including custom typography and woven jacquard
- **Packaging suppliers:** Polybags, garment bags, tissue paper, boxes, hangers
- **Fabric mills:** Raw greige fabric through to finished coated and printed fabric in MOQs typically starting at 500–1,000 metres per colourway
- **Embroidery and printing sub-contractors:** Screen print, digital print, embroidery patch, heat transfer suppliers with or without MOQ minimums depending on technique

These upstream suppliers are often the right first stop for a brand that wants to lock in materials before approaching garment factories.

### OEM vs. ODM

Understanding this distinction is foundational for brand founders. Both terms describe the manufacturing relationship, not the product quality.

**ODM — Original Design Manufacturing**

The factory has designed the product. You purchase the design with minimal modification — typically your logo, a colour change, and your packaging. The factory owns the underlying IP.

- Walk the Canton Fair floor and you are looking at ODM products
- Lower development cost — no tooling or pattern fees
- Faster to market — existing patterns, graded sizes, approved fabric
- Lower MOQ is more feasible because the factory is already in production
- Differentiation is difficult — your competitors may source the identical product from the same factory

**OEM — Original Equipment Manufacturing**

You supply the design (tech pack, fit sample, material specifications) and the factory executes it. You own the design IP; the factory provides manufacturing labour and capability.

- Higher development cost — expect sample development fees of $50–$300 per style, plus pattern grading
- Longer lead time — first sample iteration typically 3–6 weeks; multiple rounds common
- Strong IP ownership if backed by a properly written manufacturing agreement
- True product differentiation — competitors cannot source the same style
- Higher MOQs typically required (500–2,000 pieces per style) because factory setup is bespoke

**Practical guidance by order volume:**

| Volume tier | Recommended model |
|---|---|
| Under 500 units/style | ODM — use factory's existing design, add your branding |
| 500–2,000 units/style | OEM with factory's pattern base adapted to your spec |
| 2,000+ units/style | Full OEM with custom tech packs |

## 4. Sourcing Mechanics Brand Founders Must Understand

### MOQ — Minimum Order Quantities

MOQ is the primary barrier for early-stage brand founders. Ranges vary significantly by supplier type, product complexity, and factory size.

| Supplier / product type | Typical MOQ range |
|---|---|
| Large Guangdong factory (full-package) | 500–1,000 pieces per style per colourway |
| Medium factory (specialised apparel) | 200–500 pieces per style |
| Small factory / CMT | 50–200 pieces per style |
| Trading company | Often 100–300 pieces — more negotiable |
| Accessories (bags, hats) | 200–500 pieces per SKU |
| Labels and trimmings | 500–1,000 pieces per design minimum for woven |
| Fabric mills | 500–1,000 metres per colourway |

MOQs stated at Canton Fair booths are negotiable, especially for:
- Buyers who demonstrate a long-term pipeline
- Orders offering payment within 3–5 days of PO confirmation
- Buyers willing to pay a small premium per unit in exchange for lower volume
- ODM orders where no new tooling or pattern work is required

### Lead Times

| Stage | Typical timeframe |
|---|---|
| Proto/first sample (new development) | 2–4 weeks |
| Fit sample revision round | 1–2 weeks per iteration |
| Salesman sample (bulk pre-production) | 2–3 weeks after approved fit sample |
| Pre-production sample approval | 1–2 weeks (buyer review) |
| Bulk production (standard complexity) | 4–8 weeks after PO and fabric in-house |
| Bulk production (complex styles, outerwear) | 8–12 weeks |
| Sea freight (China → US West Coast) | 18–25 days |
| Sea freight (China → UK/EU) | 25–35 days |
| Air freight (China → US/EU) | 3–7 days |

Total timeline from first contact to goods in-market: typically 3–6 months for a new development, 2–4 months for repeat orders or ODM.

Critical path pinch points:
- Fabric lead time (if fabric must be sourced from a separate mill — common for custom prints or specialty fabrics)
- Trim procurement (particularly branded hardware with long tooling timelines)
- Seasonal capacity — Chinese New Year (January/February) causes 3–6 week factory shutdowns across the industry

### Price Negotiation Levers

Factors that drive unit price (in rough order of impact):

1. **Order quantity** — the single largest lever; doubling volume often reduces unit cost 10–20%
2. **Fabric specification** — switching from a custom fabric to a stock fabric can reduce cost 15–30%
3. **Construction complexity** — seam count, pocket types, lining, bonding, hardware all add cost
4. **Number of colourways** — consolidating SKUs into fewer colours reduces dye lot setup costs
5. **Trim and label spec** — woven labels cost more than printed; metal hardware costs more than plastic
6. **Payment terms** — offering faster payment (full deposit rather than 30/70 split) can unlock small discounts
7. **Exclusivity** — committing to a seasonal volume across multiple styles can unlock lower per-unit pricing

Benchmarking before Canton Fair is essential — arriving without a target price range makes negotiation difficult.

### Sample Fees and Development Costs

At the Canton Fair, physical samples on display at booths are often offered free-to-take. However, custom sample development carries costs:

| Development item | Typical cost range |
|---|---|
| Proto sample (factory's existing block, minor mod) | $0–$80 per style |
| Custom proto sample (your tech pack, new pattern) | $80–$300 per style |
| Fabric swatch / lab dip | $0–$30 per colourway |
| Shipping sample to buyer | $30–$100 via DHL/FedEx (buyer typically pays) |
| Pre-production / salesman sample | Usually 2x–3x unit cost (refundable against bulk order at many factories) |
| Embroidery / print strike-off | $20–$60 per design |

Sample fee waivers are common when buyers can demonstrate a credible bulk order intent. Many factories will absorb sample costs if the first bulk order clears a certain value threshold (typically $5,000–$20,000 USD).

### Payment Terms

**Standard terms (most Canton Fair suppliers):**

- **30% T/T deposit** — paid to start production; factory orders fabric and begins cutting
- **70% T/T balance** — paid before goods leave China (either pre-shipment or against Bill of Lading)

**Buyer-protective alternatives (negotiable for established relationships):**

- 20% deposit / 50% on production completion and QC pass / 30% after delivery
- Letter of Credit (L/C) — standard for large orders (>$50,000); shifts risk but requires bank involvement and adds cost ($500–$1,500 per transaction)

**Payment method mechanics:**
- Wire transfer (T/T) in USD is the universal default
- Alibaba Trade Assurance can be used for online orders but is not typical for Canton Fair negotiated orders
- PayPal is accepted by some smaller suppliers but carries high fees and is disfavoured for large amounts
- Western Union: accepted for samples and small amounts only

**Red flags:**
- Supplier requests full payment upfront on a first order
- Bank account name does not match the company name on documentation
- Multiple bank accounts or frequent account changes

### Quality Control Approaches

A three-stage QC framework is industry best practice:

**1. Inline / During Production Inspection (DUPRO)**
Conducted when approximately 20–30% of production is complete and packed. Identifies fabric, cut, and construction defects early when corrections are still cheap. Cost: typically $150–$300 per man-day.

**2. Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI / Final Random Inspection)**
Conducted when 100% of production is complete and at least 80% is packed into shipping cartons. Random samples drawn per AQL/ISO 2859-1 standards. Standard acceptance criteria: 0% critical defects, 2.5% major defects, 4.0% minor defects. Cost: $150–$320 per man-day.

**3. Container Loading Supervision**
Inspector witnesses goods being loaded into the container, verifying quantity, carton condition, and correct goods being shipped.

**Third-party inspection companies operating in Guangdong:**
- SGS
- Bureau Veritas (BV)
- Intertek
- QIMA (formerly AsiaInspection)
- CTSTEK
- AQI Service

All reputable third-party inspectors deliver reports on the same day. Budget approximately $250–$350 per inspection day inclusive of travel in the Guangdong region.

**Certifications relevant to fashion sourcing:**
- OEKO-TEX Standard 100 — chemical safety in textiles
- GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard) — for organic cotton claims
- GRS (Global Recycled Standard) — for recycled fibre claims
- ISO 9001 — quality management system certification

### Shipping Basics

**Key Incoterms for apparel sourcing:**

| Term | Who arranges freight | Who pays insurance | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EXW** (Ex Works) | Buyer | Buyer | Experienced importers who want maximum control |
| **FOB** (Free on Board) | Buyer from origin port | Buyer | Most common for mid-size importers; factory handles export clearance |
| **CIF** (Cost, Insurance, Freight) | Seller | Seller | New importers; simpler but buyer loses carrier control |
| **DDP** (Delivered Duty Paid) | Seller door-to-door | Seller | Very new importers; all-inclusive but highest cost |

**FOB is the dominant Incoterm for Canton Fair–sourced apparel.** The factory loads goods onto the vessel at the port of origin (typically Guangzhou Nansha, Yantian/Shenzhen, or Shanghai). Risk and cost transfer to the buyer from that point.

Under FOB, the FOB price formula is: Materials + Labour + Overhead + Commission + Local Transport to Port.

**Shipping modes:**

| Mode | Transit time (China → US) | Cost per unit (apparel) | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sea freight (FCL — full container) | 18–25 days (West Coast) | $0.50–$1.50 | Large orders, seasonal stock |
| Sea freight (LCL — groupage) | 25–35 days | $1.50–$3.00 | Mid-size orders, mixed SKUs |
| Air freight | 3–7 days | $5.00–$10.00+ | Urgent replenishment, samples, high-value items |
| Air express (DHL/FedEx/UPS) | 2–5 days | $10.00–$20.00+ | Samples only |

Key ports: Guangzhou (Nansha), Yantian (Shenzhen), Shanghai (major hub), Ningbo. Most Guangdong garment factories default to Yantian or Nansha.

## 5. Digital Sourcing Alternatives to Canton Fair

### Platform Comparison

| Platform | Primary audience | Price level | Language | Fashion/apparel depth | Trust mechanisms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Alibaba.com** | International buyers | Medium–High | English | Strong | Trade Assurance, Gold Supplier badges |
| **1688.com** | Domestic Chinese buyers | Low (factory-gate) | Chinese only | Very strong | Alipay, domestic ratings |
| **Made-in-China.com** | International B2B | Medium | English | Moderate | Verified factory badges |
| **Global Sources** | International B2B | Medium–High | English | Strong for accessories | Trade shows + online |
| **Canton Fair Online** | Canton Fair exhibitors | Medium | English | Broad | Fair organisers' platform |

### Alibaba.com

The dominant international sourcing platform. Alibaba's Trade Assurance program holds payment until order specifications are confirmed as met, making it safer for first-time buyers. Pricing is typically 20–30% higher than 1688 due to export markups and the prevalence of trading company listings. Best for: early-stage founders, mixed-category sourcing, or buyers who need English-language support. Search strategy: filter for "Gold Supplier" 5+ years, verified business, and "Trade Assurance" enabled; then cross-reference against 1688 to benchmark pricing.

### 1688.com

Alibaba's domestic Chinese marketplace. Factory-gate pricing — often 20–40% cheaper than equivalent Alibaba listings for the same factory. Limitations: Chinese-language only interface, no Trade Assurance, China-only logistics (requires a domestic agent or freight forwarder to handle export). Practical workflow: use a sourcing agent or freight consolidator with a 1688 purchasing service. Best for: brands with an established sourcing agent relationship or those comfortable with higher operational complexity.

### Made-in-China.com

Export-focused B2B directory with strong emphasis on factory verification, certifications, and industrial categories. Useful for: locating verified manufacturers by product type, particularly for more technical apparel items like outerwear, workwear, or performance fabrics. Less browsed for fashion-forward garments than Alibaba.

### Global Sources

Competes with Alibaba but positions itself at the premium verified-supplier end. Notable for: fashion accessories (bags, jewellery, belts), electronics, and product categories where certification and compliance documentation matters. Also operates trade shows in Hong Kong (Global Sources Fashion, Global Sources Electronics) that complement Canton Fair attendance.

### Canton Fair Online Platform

The official online extension of the physical Canton Fair. Exhibitors maintain year-round storefronts. Useful for: pre-fair research (identifying booths to target), post-fair follow-up if a business card is lost, and sourcing outside fair dates. However, it functions like a less-optimised version of Alibaba and is most valuable as a supplement to in-person attendance, not a replacement.

### Where Digital Platforms Sit in a Typical Sourcing Workflow

```
Phase 1 — Research & Benchmarking (before Canton Fair)
  → Alibaba / Global Sources to identify supplier categories, benchmark pricing, understand MOQs

Phase 2 — Canton Fair In-Person Sourcing
  → Physical sample inspection, face-to-face negotiation, relationship building
  → Visiting multiple suppliers in the same category in one day — impossible online

Phase 3 — Post-Fair Qualification & Development
  → 1688 cross-referencing for price verification
  → Sample orders via Alibaba Trade Assurance for new suppliers not yet trusted
  → Direct T/T for established supplier relationships

Phase 4 — Ongoing Reorders
  → Direct supplier contact (WeChat / email)
  → 1688 for price benchmarking when renegotiating
```

The in-person Canton Fair has a structural price advantage over digital platforms: you are often negotiating with senior factory management rather than export sales reps, and factories price the fair as a marketing investment rather than a margin extraction exercise.

## 6. Common Pain Points and Mistakes for First-Time Canton Fair Buyers

### Attending the Wrong Phase

The most costly mistake. Clothing and textiles are exclusively in **Phase 3** (May 1–5 spring / October 31–November 4 autumn). Arriving for Phase 1 (electronics) or Phase 2 (home décor/furniture) means zero relevant suppliers.

### Arriving Without a Sourcing Brief

The fair spans 1.55 million square metres and 74,000+ booths. Buyers who arrive without a defined product brief, target price range, MOQ ceiling, and preferred materials spend most of their time wandering. Prepare a one-page sourcing brief before you land — it also enables productive supplier conversations within the first 30 seconds of a meeting.

### Failing to Research Exhibitors in Advance

The official Canton Fair app and website publish exhibitor lists and booth numbers 4–6 weeks before the fair. Map your top 15–20 target suppliers before you arrive, book appointments where possible, and send pre-arrival emails. Top-tier suppliers book meetings with serious buyers in advance; walk-in conversations get less senior attention.

### Trusting Booth Appearance Without Verification

Many booths look impressive regardless of actual factory capability. Signs of a credible factory vs. a shell operation include: ability to answer technical production questions without consulting "the team"; willingness to share business licence, export records, and factory video; clear answer to "how many machines do you run and what is your monthly capacity?" Trading companies often stumble on capacity questions.

### Focusing Exclusively on Price

The cheapest quote at the fair often signals: lower-grade fabric, hidden trim substitutions, skipped QC steps, or a factory that will reprice when you reorder. Evaluate suppliers on total cost of quality — unit price, defect rate, lead time reliability, and communication responsiveness — not unit price alone.

### Not Negotiating MOQs

Stated MOQs are opening positions, not fixed constraints. First-time buyers often accept published MOQs without question. In practice, showing genuine long-term volume intent, offering faster payment, or accepting a small price premium per unit regularly unlocks MOQ reductions of 30–50%.

### Skipping the Sample-First Protocol

Placing a bulk order based on a fair booth sample — without ordering and approving a factory-made production sample — is a common and expensive mistake. The booth sample may be a one-off made in ideal conditions. Always receive and approve a pre-production sample (PP sample) before releasing the production deposit.

### Not Getting Everything in Writing

Verbal agreements at the fair on price, MOQ, lead time, and specifications are routinely forgotten or reinterpreted. Before leaving a supplier's booth, photograph all quoted terms. Follow up within 48 hours with a written summary email; ask the supplier to confirm in reply. Use a formal Purchase Order with payment terms, lead time commitments, spec references, and penalty clauses for late delivery before transferring any deposit.

### Ignoring Labelling and Compliance Requirements

Garments for the US market require: fibre content labels, country of origin, care instructions (per FTC rules). EU market requires: REACH compliance, CE marking for children's wear, EN 14682 (drawstring standards). Failing to specify these requirements before production results in either non-compliant goods or expensive relabelling.

### Poor Hotel and Logistics Planning

During Canton Fair season, Guangzhou hotels near Pazhou inflate 30–80% and can book out 4–6 weeks in advance. Book early. Use Metro Line 8 (Pazhou stop, exit B) — it is faster and more reliable than taxis during peak fair hours when surrounding roads are gridlocked.

### Neglecting Post-Fair Follow-Up

Good suppliers are sought after. Sending a generic "nice to meet you" email 2 weeks after the fair is insufficient. Best practice: email within 24–48 hours with a specific sample request or RFQ, reference the exact product and spec discussed, and state your expected order timeline. Suppliers prioritise buyers who demonstrate readiness to convert.

### Over-relying on a Single Supplier

Diversifying across at least two suppliers per product category from the start de-risks production delays, quality inconsistencies, and capacity constraints during peak seasons. This also provides price benchmarking leverage when renegotiating.

## 7. Practical Registration and Logistics Reference

### Registering as a Buyer (139th Canton Fair, 2026)

Pre-registration opens February 14, 2026.

1. Register at cantonfair.org.cn — select "Overseas Buyer"
2. Apply for a Buyer Entry Badge (free for pre-registered overseas buyers)
3. Apply for an official invitation letter (needed for Chinese visa applications)
4. On arrival: present passport, pre-registration receipt, and business card at the overseas registration counter
5. Badge collection also available at 31 hotel-based registration offices across Guangzhou

On-site first-time registration (without pre-registration): 100 RMB (~$15 USD). Late registration: ~200 RMB.

The buyer badge works across multiple sessions and years once issued.

### Getting to the Venue

- Metro: Line 8, Pazhou station (Exit B connects directly to the complex)
- The complex has three halls/zones across both sides of Xingang East Road — confirm which zone your target exhibitors are in before departure
