## 1. Core Fabric Categories

Fabrics divide into four construction families. Understanding this taxonomy first helps map supplier capabilities — most Canton Fair textile mills specialise in one or two of these families.

### 1.1 Woven Fabrics

**Construction:** Two sets of yarn (warp + weft) interlaced at right angles on a loom. The resulting structure is dimensionally stable with little to no inherent stretch unless elastomeric yarns are included.

**Key weave structures:**

| Weave | Characteristics | Typical Fabrics |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain weave** | Simplest interlacing; balanced, breathable | Poplin, muslin, chambray, chiffon, organza |
| **Twill weave** | Diagonal rib; durable, drapes well | Denim, gabardine, tweed, chino |
| **Satin weave** | Long yarn floats; smooth, lustrous surface | Satin, charmeuse, duchess satin |
| **Dobby / Jacquard** | Complex pattern woven in; raised texture | Brocade, damask, piqué |
| **Canvas / Duck** | Tight plain or basket weave; very firm | Outerwear shells, bags, workwear |

**Strengths:** Structure, durability, print clarity, tailoring precision.
**Limitations:** Less stretch than knits; generally higher production cost.
**Canton Fair presence:** Very high — wovens dominate garment and home-textile halls.

---

### 1.2 Knit Fabrics

**Construction:** Interlocking loops of yarn. Inherently stretchy because the loop structure compresses and extends with body movement, even without elastic fibres.

**Key knit structures:**

| Type | Sub-types | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Weft knit** | Jersey, rib, interlock, French terry, fleece | Horizontal loops; stretches widthwise; most T-shirts and casualwear |
| **Warp knit** | Tricot, mesh, power net | Vertical loops; more stable; used in lingerie, sportswear linings |
| **Circular knit** | Single jersey, double jersey | Seamless tube construction; mass-market efficiency |
| **Flat knit** | Fully-fashioned knitwear | Shaped panels for sweaters; less waste |

**Strengths:** Comfort, stretch, lower cost in mass production, fast manufacturing.
**Limitations:** Can snag, curl at edges; less suited to sharp tailoring.
**Canton Fair presence:** Extremely high — activewear, casualwear, and underwear halls are dominated by knit suppliers.

---

### 1.3 Non-Woven Fabrics

**Construction:** Fibres bonded by heat, chemical, or mechanical means (needlepunching, spunbonding, felting) — not interlaced or looped.

**Key types:**

| Type | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Felt** | Coat interlinings, craft, structured accessories |
| **Interfacing / Interlining** | Collar stiffening, waistband support, bag structure |
| **Spunbond / SMS** | Linings, disposable protective garments |
| **Needlepunch** | Industrial textiles, insulation batting |
| **Fusible web** | Bonding layers in tailored garments |

**Strengths:** Inexpensive, no grain direction, good for structure and padding.
**Limitations:** Not breathable; limited drape; typically not used as outer fabric.
**Canton Fair presence:** Moderate — primarily in accessories, interlining, and industrial halls.

---

### 1.4 Technical Fabrics

Engineered textiles where performance function (not just appearance) drives construction choices. See Section 5 for full detail.

**Subcategories relevant to apparel:**
- Moisture-wicking / moisture-management
- UV-protective (UPF-rated)
- Waterproof / water-resistant (DWR, laminated, membrane)
- Compression and 4-way stretch
- Thermal / insulating (hollow-fibre fill, fleece, Primaloft)
- Antimicrobial / odour-resistant
- Flame-retardant (FR)
- Smart / e-textiles (sensors, conductive fibres — emerging)

## 2. Natural Fibres

Natural fibres are biodegradable, breathable, and carry strong consumer appeal. Premium quality tiers exist for nearly every natural fibre — important for app differentiation.

---

### 2.1 Cotton

**Origin:** Seed hair of *Gossypium* plant; largest volume textile crop globally.
**Primary growing regions:** China, India, USA, Brazil, Pakistan.

**Key properties:**
- Soft, breathable, hypoallergenic
- High moisture absorption (absorbs up to 27× its weight)
- Easy to dye; good colour retention
- Machine washable; strong when wet
- Prone to shrinkage, wrinkling, and mildew if not finished

**Fabric constructions from cotton:**
Jersey, poplin, twill, canvas, denim, terry, voile, corduroy, velvet, fleece (cotton-backed)

**Typical weights (GSM) by application:**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Lightweight shirts / blouses | 80–130 |
| Standard T-shirts | 150–185 |
| Premium heavyweight T-shirts | 200–260 |
| Poplin shirting | 100–140 |
| Chino / trouser fabric | 200–260 |
| Denim (shirting) | 180–220 |
| Denim (jeans) | 300–420 |
| Hoodies / sweatshirts (fleece) | 280–380 |
| Canvas / outerwear shell | 350–500 |

**Quality tiers:**
- **Commodity cotton** — standard staple length (~25 mm); most fast-fashion
- **Combed cotton** — short fibres removed; smoother, stronger, softer
- **Ring-spun cotton** — tighter twist; premium hand feel; slower to pill
- **Pima / Supima cotton** — extra-long staple (~38 mm); USA-grown; luxury shirting and knitwear
- **Egyptian cotton** — extra-long staple; high lustre; luxury bedding and shirting

**Sustainability notes:** Conventional cotton uses ~10,000 litres of water per kg; heavy pesticide use. Organic, BCI (Better Cotton Initiative), and Fair Trade certifications signal responsible sourcing.

**Sourcing/pricing signals (China FOB):**
- Basic cotton poplin / jersey: USD 0.50–1.50/m
- Combed ring-spun jersey: USD 1.50–3.00/m
- Premium Pima/Supima sourced and made in China: rare — typically imported and re-manufactured
- MOQ: 300–1,000 m per colour for stock fabrics; 1,000–3,000 m for custom constructions

---

### 2.2 Wool

**Origin:** Fleece of sheep (*Ovis aries*); also includes specialty wools from other animals (see 2.5 Cashmere, 2.6 Alpaca).
**Primary growing regions:** Australia, New Zealand, China, Argentina, South Africa.

**Key properties:**
- Excellent thermal regulation (warm in cold, cool in warmth)
- Natural crimp creates air pockets — lightweight warmth
- Inherently moisture-wicking and odour-resistant
- Flame-resistant naturally
- Can be scratchy at coarser micron counts (>24 microns)
- Felts irreversibly if agitated when wet (unless treated — "Superwash")

**Key wool types:**

| Type | Micron | Characteristics | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coarse wool** | 30–40+ µm | Rough handle | Rugs, industrial |
| **Lambswool** | 25–30 µm | First shearing; soft | Knitwear, scarves |
| **Merino (standard)** | 22–24 µm | Soft, versatile | Knitwear, base layers |
| **Fine merino** | 17–21 µm | Very soft, next-to-skin | Premium base layers, shirting |
| **Ultra-fine merino** | <17 µm | Luxury grade | High-end knitwear, tailoring |
| **Tropical / summer wool** | varies | Lightweight suiting weave | Summer suits |
| **Boiled / felted wool** | varies | Dense, windproof | Coats, outerwear |

**Typical weights (GSM):**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Summer/tropical suiting | 180–230 |
| Year-round suiting | 240–300 |
| Knitwear (fine gauge) | 150–250 |
| Knitwear (chunky) | 300–500 |
| Overcoating / heavy outerwear | 400–600 |

**Sustainability notes:** Wool is biodegradable and renewable. Mulesing (surgical skin removal on Merinos to prevent flystrike) is a welfare concern — look for "mulesing-free" certification (ZQ, RWS — Responsible Wool Standard).

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Most raw Merino wool comes from Australia/New Zealand; Chinese manufacturers import fibre and spin/weave locally. Expect premium pricing — suiting wool at USD 5–25+/m FOB depending on grade. MOQs: typically 300–500 m for woven wool; lower for knit yardage.

---

### 2.3 Silk

**Origin:** Protein filament produced by *Bombyx mori* silkworm cocoons.
**Primary growing regions:** China (dominant, ~80% of global supply), India, Thailand, Brazil.

**Key properties:**
- Exceptional lustre and drape — the benchmark for luxury hand-feel
- Strongest natural fibre by weight
- Temperature-regulating (cool in summer, warm in winter)
- Hypoallergenic; smooth texture suits sensitive skin
- Absorbs moisture without feeling damp
- Weakens with prolonged sun exposure; requires careful laundering
- High cost — labour-intensive production

**Key silk fabric types:**

| Fabric | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| **Charmeuse** | Satin-weave; lustrous face, dull back; soft drape |
| **Chiffon** | Plain weave; sheer, lightweight, floaty |
| **Crêpe de chine (CDC)** | Subtle texture; matte finish; more structured drape |
| **Dupioni / Shantung** | Slubbed texture; crisp; more structured |
| **Habotai (China silk)** | Lightest weight; linings, scarves |
| **Organza** | Sheer, crisp; structured skirts, overlays |
| **Twill silk** | Diagonal weave (e.g., Hermès-style scarves) |
| **Velvet (silk)** | Pile on silk base; luxurious eveningwear |

**Typical weights (GSM):**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Linings / habotai | 60–90 |
| Chiffon / organza | 40–80 |
| Charmeuse / CDC | 100–160 |
| Dupioni / heavier silk | 100–180 |

**Quality tiers:**
- Measured in **momme (mm)** — weight of 100 yards × 45" wide in pounds. Higher momme = heavier, more durable.
- 6–8 mm: very lightweight, scarves
- 12–16 mm: standard charmeuse / CDC for garments
- 19–25 mm: heavier charmeuse, durable shirting
- 30+ mm: luxury upholstery / structured gowns

**Sustainability notes:** Silk production kills silkworms — "peace silk" (non-violent) allows worms to emerge before degumming. Ahimsa silk is a premium ethical alternative.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** China is the world's leading silk manufacturer — Suzhou and Hangzhou are major silk hubs. FOB prices: USD 3–20+/m depending on weight and type. MOQ: typically 100–300 m for stock; 500+ m for custom weaves.

---

### 2.4 Linen

**Origin:** Bast fibre from flax plant (*Linum usitatissimum*).
**Primary growing regions:** France, Belgium, Netherlands (premium European); China, Egypt.

**Key properties:**
- Strong and durable — gets stronger when wet
- Highly breathable; excellent moisture absorption and quick dry
- Naturally thermo-regulating; cool in heat
- Resistant to dirt, stains, lint, and pilling
- Becomes softer and more supple with washing and wear
- Wrinkles easily (a desirable character for many consumers; can be treated)
- Relatively inexpensive at commodity grade

**Fabric constructions from linen:**
Plain weave (most common), twill, slub weave, linen-cotton blends, linen-silk blends

**Typical weights (GSM):**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Sheer / voile weight shirting | 80–120 |
| Summer shirts, blouses | 120–160 |
| Trousers, skirts | 160–210 |
| Structured shirting / suiting | 200–260 |
| Upholstery / heavy outerwear | 250–400 |

**Quality tiers:**
- **European (French/Belgian) flax** — long staple, fine line fibres; premium hand feel; OEKO-TEX certified
- **Chinese linen** — shorter staple; less refined; cost-effective; widely available at Canton Fair
- **Egyptian linen** — extra-long staple; smooth; mid-tier pricing

**Sustainability notes:** Flax requires minimal irrigation and pesticides; entire plant is used with little waste. European flax is among the most sustainable textile crops. Look for OEKO-TEX or Masters of Linen certification.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Chinese linen (often blended with cotton or viscose for softness) widely available at Canton Fair. Pure linen: USD 1.50–6.00/m; linen-cotton blends: USD 1.00–3.50/m. MOQ: 100–500 m for stock; 500–2,000 m for custom.

---

### 2.5 Cashmere

**Origin:** Fine undercoat of Cashmere goat (*Capra hircus laniger*).
**Primary growing regions:** China (Inner Mongolia), Mongolia, Afghanistan, Iran, Nepal.

**Key properties:**
- Exceptionally soft — finest animal fibre available at scale
- 6–8× warmer than sheep wool at equivalent weight
- Very lightweight despite warmth
- Minimal itchiness or allergic reaction
- Fibre is fine and delicate — prone to pilling if not blended or well-constructed
- High cost — each goat yields only 150–200 g of usable fibre per year

**Typical weights (GSM):**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Lightweight knitwear (fine gauge) | 150–220 |
| Mid-weight sweaters | 250–350 |
| Cashmere scarves / wraps | 100–200 |
| Cashmere coats / outerwear | 350–500 |

**Quality tiers (by fibre diameter):**
- **Grade A (premium):** 14–16 µm — ultra-soft, minimal pilling; top luxury brands
- **Grade B:** 16–18 µm — standard luxury; most premium high-street
- **Grade C:** 18–19 µm — value cashmere; may blend with wool
- **Blended cashmere:** Often mixed with merino (adds weight/durability) or silk (adds sheen)

**Sustainability notes:** Overgrazing by cashmere goats is a documented cause of desertification in Mongolia. Responsible Cashmere Round Table (RCRT) and Good Cashmere Standard (GCS) are key certifications. Demand is growing for certified traceable cashmere.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** China (particularly Inner Mongolia) is the world's largest cashmere producer and processor. FOB prices for cashmere fabric: USD 20–80+/m depending on grade and weight. Yarn-forward production common. MOQ: 50–200 m for woven; lower for knitwear yardage. Verify grade rigorously — blends are often mis-labeled.

---

### 2.6 Alpaca

**Origin:** Fleece of domesticated alpaca (*Vicugna pacos*); two breeds — Huacaya (fluffy, crimped) and Suri (silky, straight locks).
**Primary growing regions:** Peru (~80% of global supply), Bolivia, Chile, Ecuador.

**Key properties:**
- Lighter and warmer than sheep's wool
- Hollow-core fibre traps heat efficiently — superior warmth-to-weight ratio
- Naturally hypoallergenic — no lanolin (unlike sheep wool)
- Resistant to water, odours, and fire
- Silky lustre, especially Suri type
- 22+ natural colour shades — can be worn undyed
- Softer than most wool at equivalent micron count

**Quality tiers:**
- **Royal alpaca:** <19 µm — ultra-fine, rare; highest price
- **Baby alpaca:** 20–22 µm — first shearing or softest body sections; luxury grade
- **Fine alpaca:** 22–25 µm — standard premium
- **Standard alpaca:** 25–30 µm — everyday knitwear

**Key fabric types:**
- Huacaya knit: fluffy, soft jumpers, scarves
- Suri woven: lustrous suiting and outerwear fabric
- Alpaca-wool or alpaca-silk blends: adds weight/structure

**Typical uses:** Luxury knitwear, scarves, wraps, fine coats, premium suiting (Suri)

**Sustainability notes:** Low carbon footprint vs. cattle/sheep; minimal pesticide use; naturally coloured fibres reduce dyeing impact. Responsible Alpaca Standard (RAS) covers animal welfare. Sourcing is primarily from South America — less represented at Canton Fair (Chinese manufacturers may import fibre).

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Less common at Canton Fair than wool or cashmere — more typical in South American and Italian supply chains. Alpaca fabric FOB (when available from Chinese processors): USD 15–60+/m. Verify fibre origin and blend ratio carefully.

## 3. Synthetic Fibres

Synthetics are manufactured from petrochemicals (or, in the case of semi-synthetics, from processed natural cellulose). They dominate volume in mass-market fashion and are heavily represented at Canton Fair — especially polyester and nylon in knit and woven constructions.

---

### 3.1 Polyester

**Origin:** Derived from petroleum via polymerisation of ethylene glycol and terephthalic acid (PET).

**Key properties:**
- Extremely durable and abrasion-resistant
- Wrinkle-resistant, shape-retentive
- Quick-drying; low moisture absorption
- Colour-fast; good UV resistance
- Resists shrinkage and stretching
- Lightweight; easy care (machine wash/tumble dry)
- Poor breathability at high GSM
- Sheds microplastics when washed
- Takes 200+ years to decompose

**Common constructions:**
Woven (taffeta, satin, chiffon, twill, georgette), knit (jersey, interlock, mesh), non-woven (interlining)

**Common blends:**
- **Polyester/Cotton (TC or PC)** — TC = 65% poly / 35% cotton; balances durability with breathability. Workhorse of uniform and casualwear.
- **Polyester/Viscose (TR)** — Soft drape with structure; suiting and workwear.
- **Polyester/Spandex (85/15, 80/20)** — Activewear standard; stretch + recovery.
- **Polyester/Linen-look** — Linen texture at synthetic price point.

**Typical weights (GSM):**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Linings, chiffon | 50–80 |
| Activewear tops | 130–180 |
| Swimwear | 180–240 |
| Suiting / TR fabric | 200–300 |
| Outerwear / shell | 100–200 (tightly woven) |

**Sustainability notes:** Recycled polyester (rPET) cuts energy 59% and CO2 32% vs. virgin. Main concern is microplastic shedding — Guppyfriend bags and washing filters are emerging mitigation tools.

**Sourcing/pricing signals (China FOB):**
- Basic polyester woven: USD 0.30–0.70/m
- Polyester chiffon / georgette: USD 0.50–1.50/m
- Performance knit (poly/spandex): USD 1.00–3.50/m
- Custom prints on polyester: USD 0.80–2.50/m (MOQ typically 500 m per design)
- Very low MOQs possible for stock fabrics: 50–100 m

---

### 3.2 Nylon (Polyamide)

**Origin:** Fully synthetic polyamide polymer; PA6 and PA6.6 are the most common apparel grades.

**Key properties:**
- Strongest synthetic fibre — excellent abrasion resistance
- Lightweight and flexible; dyes well
- Good elasticity and recovery
- Quick-drying; near-waterproof naturally
- Smooth surface: comfortable next-to-skin
- More expensive than polyester
- Sensitive to UV (yellows over time without UV stabilisers)
- Melts rather than burns — fire safety consideration

**Common constructions:**
Tricot knit (lingerie), ripstop woven (outerwear, bags), taffeta, mesh

**Common blends:**
- **Nylon/Spandex (80/20, 78/22)** — Swimwear standard: chlorine-resistant stretch
- **Nylon/Wool** — Sock yarns; adds durability without added bulk
- **Nylon/Polyester** — Outerwear shells

**Typical weights (GSM):**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Hosiery / tights | 15–40 |
| Lingerie / swimwear | 140–220 |
| Athletic/yoga wear | 150–230 |
| Ripstop outerwear shell | 50–150 |

**Sustainability notes:** ECONYL (Aquafil) is recycled nylon made from fishing nets and ocean waste — growing presence in swimwear and activewear. Bluesign and GRS (Global Recycled Standard) are key certifications.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Nylon is more expensive than polyester but still very accessible in China. Nylon fabric FOB: USD 1.50–5.00/m for knits; ripstop and wovens vary. MOQ: 100–500 m.

---

### 3.3 Acrylic

**Origin:** Synthetic polymer (polyacrylonitrile) — developed as a wool alternative.

**Key properties:**
- Soft, lightweight, woolly hand feel
- Good thermal insulation — warmer than cotton at equivalent weight
- Machine washable (unlike wool); shape-retentive
- Resistant to moths, chemicals, and UV
- Dyes easily; colour-fast
- Low moisture absorption — can feel clammy in warm weather
- Prone to pilling
- Poor environmental profile — not biodegradable; microplastic shedding

**Common constructions:**
Knit (sweaters, cardigans, fleece), fake fur, pile fabrics

**Common blends:**
- **Acrylic/Wool** — reduces cost and itch; common in budget knitwear
- **Acrylic/Cotton** — adds warmth to cotton base
- **Acrylic/Modacrylic** — fake fur construction

**Typical uses:** Budget knitwear, hats, socks, fleece jackets, imitation fur trim

**Sustainability notes:** Poor sustainability profile — not biodegradable and significant microplastic shedder. Consider as a value tier with transparency messaging.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Very inexpensive. Acrylic yarn and fabric are widely available from Chinese suppliers. Knit acrylic fabric: USD 0.80–2.50/m. MOQ: low (50–200 m for stock).

---

### 3.4 Spandex / Elastane / Lycra

**Origin:** Polyurethane-based synthetic; brand name Lycra owned by Lycra Company (formerly DuPont). "Elastane" is the generic European term; "spandex" is generic US term.

**Key properties:**
- Can stretch 5–8× its length and recover fully
- Very lightweight — typically used in small percentages (2–20%)
- Smooth; comfortable next-to-skin
- Chlorine- and heat-sensitive (degrades swimwear over time)
- Never used alone — always blended

**Typical blend percentages and applications:**

| Blend | Spandex % | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Cotton/spandex | 2–5% | Stretch jeans, shirts — minimal stretch |
| Jersey/spandex | 5–10% | T-shirts, casual stretch |
| Legging / yoga fabric | 15–25% | High stretch, compression |
| Swimwear (nylon/spandex) | 18–22% | Chlorine-resistant 4-way stretch |
| Power net / compression | 20–30% | Shapewear, sports bras |

**Sustainability notes:** Polyurethane-based; not biodegradable. Bio-based spandex (e.g., DuPont's bio-PDO-based Sorona) is emerging. Current recycling of spandex-blended fabrics is difficult — a significant circular economy barrier.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Spandex is a component, not a standalone fabric. It adds approximately USD 0.30–1.00/m to fabric cost vs. equivalent non-stretch version. Chinese manufacturers offer the full range of spandex-blended knits. Very accessible.

---

### 3.5 Viscose / Rayon

**Origin:** Semi-synthetic cellulosic fibre — cellulose (wood pulp, bamboo, sugarcane) is dissolved and extruded into filaments. Viscose = European term; Rayon = US term.

**Key properties:**
- Silk-like drape and lustre — most "silk-look" garments are viscose
- Breathable and moisture-absorbent
- Soft next-to-skin; comfortable in warm weather
- Dyes easily — vibrant colour results
- Relatively weak — especially when wet; prone to shrinking
- Wrinkles easily
- Not inherently sustainable — production involves carbon disulfide (toxic chemical) in conventional process

**Key viscose variants:**

| Variant | Process | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard viscose** | Conventional wet spinning | Soft, drapes well; most common |
| **Modal** | High-wet-modulus viscose | Stronger when wet; softer; less prone to shrinkage |
| **Lyocell (TENCEL)** | Closed-loop solvent | Sustainable; strong; moisture-wicking (see Section 4) |
| **Bamboo viscose** | Conventional process on bamboo pulp | Often marketed as "eco" but process is same as standard |
| **Cupro** | Copper ammonium solution | Very silky; luxury lining alternative |

**Common blends:**
- **Viscose/Polyester** — adds structure and durability to drape
- **Viscose/Nylon** — lingerie, blouses
- **Viscose/Linen** — softens linen; reduces wrinkling
- **Cotton/Viscose** — casual wear; improves drape over 100% cotton

**Typical weights (GSM):**

| Application | GSM |
|---|---|
| Chiffon / crepe (viscose) | 80–130 |
| Jersey / casual knitwear | 130–200 |
| Woven shirting / blouse | 100–160 |
| Linings | 60–100 |

**Sustainability notes:** Standard viscose process is chemically intensive and polluting. Look for FSC-certified wood pulp and closed-loop certifications. TENCEL (Lyocell) is the sustainable alternative (see Section 4).

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Very widely available in China; one of the most common base fabrics for women's casualwear. Woven viscose: USD 0.80–3.00/m; viscose jersey: USD 1.00–2.50/m. MOQ: 100–500 m.

## 4. Sustainable & Eco Materials

Consumer demand for sustainability is reshaping sourcing priorities. This section covers materials where "eco" credentials are central to the value proposition — important for the app's filtering and supplier-matching logic.

---

### 4.1 Organic Cotton

**What it is:** Cotton grown without synthetic pesticides, herbicides, or GMO seeds; certified to GOTS (Global Organic Textile Standard) or OCS (Organic Content Standard).

**Properties vs. conventional cotton:** Essentially identical hand feel and performance — the difference is in the growing process, not the fibre itself.

**Key benefits:**
- Eliminates synthetic pesticide load — healthier for farmers and ecosystems
- Typically supports soil health through crop rotation
- Lower overall chemical burden in supply chain
- Strong consumer recognition — "organic cotton" is one of the most-searched sustainable terms

**Key certifications:**
- **GOTS** — covers organic fibre + responsible processing (dyes, worker conditions)
- **OCS** — certifies organic content only; weaker than GOTS
- **BCI (Better Cotton Initiative)** — not organic; improves conventional cotton practices

**Garment applications:** All the same as conventional cotton. Premium T-shirts, babywear (strongest organic demand), casualwear, denim.

**Sustainability notes:** Uses less synthetic input but yields per acre are lower — requiring more land. Water usage debate ongoing. Overall consensus: meaningfully better than conventional.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Premium of 15–30% over equivalent conventional cotton. Growing availability from Chinese mills with GOTS certification — verify certificate numbers at gots.net. MOQ similar to conventional.

---

### 4.2 Recycled Polyester (rPET)

**What it is:** Polyester fibre derived from post-consumer PET plastic — primarily plastic bottles (PET), but also increasingly from textile waste.

**Properties vs. virgin polyester:** Functionally equivalent in most applications. Slight differences in pilling or consistency at lower grades.

**Key benefits:**
- Reduces energy consumption by up to 59% vs. virgin polyester
- Cuts CO2 emissions by up to 32%
- Diverts plastic waste from landfill and oceans
- Drop-in replacement for virgin polyester — no new manufacturing infrastructure needed

**Key certifications:**
- **GRS (Global Recycled Standard)** — most widely recognised; covers recycled content + chain of custody
- **RCS (Recycled Claim Standard)** — content only; weaker than GRS
- **bluesign** — broader environmental/chemical safety standard

**Key limitation:** Microplastic shedding remains — rPET sheds similar volumes to virgin polyester. Does not solve the end-of-life problem.

**Garment applications:** Activewear, outerwear, swimwear, linings, fleece, bags.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Premium of 10–25% over virgin polyester. Widely available from Taiwanese and Chinese manufacturers — China has scaled rPET significantly. Many Canton Fair suppliers carry GRS-certified rPET. Verify GRS certificate at control-union.com. MOQ: similar to virgin polyester.

---

### 4.3 TENCEL™ Lyocell

**What it is:** Branded lyocell fibre produced by Lenzing AG (Austria) from sustainably sourced wood pulp (primarily eucalyptus), using a closed-loop solvent process that recovers ~99.5% of chemicals used.

**Key properties:**
- Silky soft; smooth surface
- Strong when wet (unlike standard viscose)
- Excellent moisture management — wicks and releases moisture quickly
- Naturally inhibits bacterial growth (odour-resistance)
- Biodegradable and compostable (~12 weeks in compost)
- Drapes beautifully — positioned between cotton and silk in hand feel
- More wrinkle-resistant than standard viscose

**Key variants:**
- **TENCEL Lyocell** — standard grade; most widely used
- **TENCEL Modal** — softer, more elastic; often used in intimates and loungewear
- **TENCEL Lyocell with REFIBRA technology** — incorporates cotton scraps from garment production; adds circularity story

**Garment applications:** Casual tops, dresses, activewear, intimates, loungewear, denim (lyocell-cotton blends), lining fabrics.

**Sustainability notes:** Best-in-class sustainability for man-made cellulosics. FSC/PEFC-certified wood; closed-loop process; biodegradable. Key talking point for eco-conscious consumers. However, "lyocell" without the TENCEL brand may use less responsible sourcing — verify.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Premium of 20–40% over standard viscose. Chinese manufacturers increasingly licensed to spin and weave TENCEL-branded fabrics. Look for Lenzing TENCEL certification. FOB: USD 2.00–6.00/m. MOQ: 200–500 m.

---

### 4.4 Hemp

**What it is:** Bast fibre from *Cannabis sativa* plant (low-THC industrial varieties); one of the oldest cultivated textile crops.

**Key properties:**
- Very strong and durable — one of the strongest natural fibres
- Naturally pest-resistant; grows without pesticides
- Carbon-negative growth (absorbs more CO2 than it produces)
- Improves soil health (phytoremediation)
- Breathable and moisture-absorbent
- Gets softer with washing and wear
- Historically coarse texture — modern processing (cottonised hemp) is significantly softer
- Blends well with cotton, silk, and lyocell

**Typical constructions:** Plain woven fabric (shirting, canvas), blended knits, denim-weight twill

**Garment applications:** Casualwear, activewear, workwear, denim alternatives, accessories, sustainable streetwear.

**Sustainability notes:** Exceptional sustainability credentials — minimal water, no pesticides, carbon-negative, soil-positive. Remains a strong brand storytelling material. Certifications: GOTS-certified hemp fabrics available.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Growing but still niche at Canton Fair. Primarily available from specialist Chinese mills in Yunnan and Shandong provinces. Hemp fabric FOB: USD 3.00–10.00/m. MOQ tends to be higher (500–2,000 m) due to limited supply chain scale. Blended hemp/cotton is more accessible and affordable.

---

### 4.5 Bamboo

**What it is:** Textile fibre derived from bamboo grass (*Bambusa* and related genera). Important to distinguish processing methods — they determine sustainability.

**Processing types and sustainability:**

| Type | Process | Sustainability | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bamboo viscose / rayon** | Conventional chemical (same as standard viscose) | Moderate — feedstock fast-growing, but process polluting | Soft, silky |
| **Bamboo lyocell** | Closed-loop solvent | High — similar to TENCEL | Strong, moisture-wicking |
| **Mechanically processed bamboo** | Physical retting (like linen) | Highest | Coarser texture; rarely used in apparel |

**Key properties (bamboo viscose — most common):**
- Very soft; silky hand feel
- Naturally moisture-wicking and breathable
- Naturally antimicrobial and odour-resistant
- Hypoallergenic
- UV-protective naturally
- Biodegradable (the fibre; not necessarily if chemically treated)
- Fast-growing crop — no replanting needed; minimal pesticides

**Garment applications:** T-shirts, activewear, underwear, socks, baby clothing, loungewear.

**Sustainability notes:** "Bamboo" in fabric labels almost always means bamboo viscose — the feedstock is sustainable but the process is not meaningfully different from standard viscose. True bamboo lyocell (e.g., from Litrax/Birla Cellulose) is the genuinely sustainable version. Greenwashing risk is high here — important to verify.

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Widely available from Chinese manufacturers. Bamboo jersey: USD 1.50–4.00/m. MOQ: 100–500 m. Bamboo lyocell is more premium and less widely available.

---

### 4.6 Other Emerging Sustainable Materials

These are growing in design-forward and luxury segments — less likely at Canton Fair but worth naming in the app for future-proofing:

| Material | Description | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **ECONYL (Recycled Nylon)** | Nylon regenerated from fishing nets/ocean waste | Commercially available; growing in swimwear |
| **Cupro (Bemberg)** | Recycled cotton linter cellulose; ultra-silky lining | Available; niche; premium |
| **Piñatex** | Pineapple leaf fibre leather alternative | Commercial; accessories |
| **Mycelium leather** | Mushroom-based leather alternative | Emerging; limited scale |
| **Algae-based fibres** | Experimental; sequestration focus | R&D stage |
| **Circulose / NuCycl** | Chemical recycling of cotton back to new fibre | Pilot scale; growing fast |

## 5. Technical & Performance Fabrics

Performance fabrics are engineered for function-first applications — primarily activewear, athleisure, outerwear, and technical sportswear. This is one of the highest-growth categories at Canton Fair.

---

### 5.1 Moisture-Wicking Fabrics

**How it works:** Capillary action pulls sweat from the skin surface through the fabric to the outer face, where it evaporates. Achieved via:
- Synthetic filament yarns with high surface area (polyester, nylon)
- Channel-shaped or cross-section modified fibres
- Hydrophilic chemical finishes on fabric surface
- Natural wicking: Merino wool and bamboo wick naturally

**Key specifications buyers request:**
- Moisture transport rate (AATCC 195)
- Drying rate (AATCC 199)

**Typical constructions:** Single jersey, mesh, bird's eye mesh, double knit
**Typical fibre:** 100% polyester or polyester/spandex blend
**Typical weight:** 120–180 GSM for tops; 180–240 GSM for leggings/shorts

**Common brand names / technologies:** Nike Dri-FIT (polyester microfibre), Adidas Climalite, Under Armour HeatGear, Coolmax (Lycra Company)

**Garment applications:** Gym tops, base layers, running shorts, cycling jerseys, football kits

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Standard moisture-wicking performance knit: USD 1.50–4.00/m from Chinese manufacturers. Very widely available. Confirm with AATCC test reports.

---

### 5.2 UV-Protective Fabrics (UPF)

**How it works:**
- Tight weave/knit reduces UV passage through fabric physically
- UV-absorbing chemical additives (UV stabilisers) applied to fibre or as finish
- Dark and bright colours absorb more UV than pale colours
- Wet fabrics transmit more UV — consider for swimwear

**UPF rating scale:**

| UPF Rating | UV Blocked | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| 15–24 | 93.3–95.8% | Good |
| 25–39 | 96–97.4% | Very Good |
| 40–50+ | 97.5–98%+ | Excellent |

**Key fibres for UV protection:**
- Nylon (naturally dense structure)
- Polyester with UV additives
- Polypropylene
- Merino wool (naturally protective)
- Bamboo (naturally protective + additive-treatable)

**Testing standard:** AS/NZS 4399 (Australian/NZ); AATCC 183 (US)

**Garment applications:** Swimwear, rash guards, outdoor shirts, hiking, sun-protective babywear

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** UPF-rated fabric commands a 15–30% premium over equivalent non-rated fabric. Chinese manufacturers can provide UPF test certificates — request from accredited labs (SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas). Nylon/spandex swimwear fabric (UPF 50+): USD 3.00–7.00/m.

---

### 5.3 Waterproof & Water-Resistant Fabrics

**Key distinctions:**

| Term | Definition | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Water-resistant** | Resists light rain briefly; not seam-sealed | — |
| **Waterproof** | Blocks sustained water pressure | ISO 811 / AATCC 127 — Hydrostatic head test |
| **Windproof** | Blocks wind penetration | — |
| **Breathable + waterproof** | Water can't get in; vapour can get out | MVTR (Moisture Vapour Transmission Rate) |

**Technologies:**

| Technology | Description | Breathability |
|---|---|---|
| **DWR (Durable Water Repellency)** | Fluorocarbon or C0 finish on fabric surface; water beads off | Does not affect breathability |
| **PU laminate / coating** | Polyurethane film bonded to fabric back | Low–moderate |
| **TPU membrane** | Thermoplastic polyurethane bonded layer | Moderate |
| **PTFE membrane (Gore-Tex)** | Microporous polytetrafluoroethylene; water can't enter, vapour can exit | High |
| **e-PTFE (expanded PTFE)** | Gore-Tex technology; 9 billion pores per square inch | Highest |

**Note on PFAS/PFC-free DWR:** Fluorocarbon-based DWR (C6, C8) is being phased out due to environmental persistence. C0 (fluorine-free) DWR is now the industry standard direction — key talking point for eco-conscious buyers.

**Garment applications:** Ski jackets, hiking outerwear, shell jackets, rain macs, outdoor trousers

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Basic PU-coated waterproof fabric: USD 1.50–4.00/m. 2L (shell + laminate) fabric: USD 3.00–8.00/m. 3L (bonded face/membrane/lining): USD 6.00–20.00+/m. Chinese manufacturers are competitive in 2L and 3L constructions. MOQ: 500–2,000 m for custom laminations.

---

### 5.4 Stretch & Compression Fabrics

**Stretch types:**

| Type | Direction | Construction |
|---|---|---|
| **2-way stretch** | One direction only | Standard weft knit; woven with elastane in warp only |
| **4-way stretch** | Warp and weft | Weft knit with spandex; bi-stretch woven with elastane in both directions |

**Compression fabrics:**
- Higher spandex content (20–30%) provides compression
- Graduated compression (varying pressure from ankle to thigh) requires specialised construction
- Polyamide/elastane is the premium standard (nylon/spandex); polyester/spandex is the volume standard

**Key metrics:**
- **Stretch percentage:** e.g., 50% (fabric stretches to 1.5× its resting width)
- **Recovery:** % fabric returns to original dimensions after stretch cycle
- **Compression force:** mmHg (millimetres of mercury) for medical-grade compression

**Garment applications:** Leggings, yoga pants, sports bras, cycling shorts, compression socks, swimwear, dance

**Typical fabric specs:**
- Standard legging/yoga: 80% nylon + 20% spandex or 85% polyester + 15% spandex; 200–240 GSM
- Premium compression (Lululemon-tier): 78% nylon + 22% Lycra; 230–260 GSM
- Light stretch T-shirt: 95% cotton + 5% spandex; 160–200 GSM

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Polyester/spandex performance knit: USD 2.00–5.00/m. Nylon/spandex premium grade: USD 4.00–9.00/m. Heavily represented at Canton Fair — many Guangdong mills specialise in this category. Confirm Lycra brand if requested (Lycra Company licensed).

---

### 5.5 Thermal & Insulation Fabrics

**Key technologies:**

| Material | Description | Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| **Fleece** | Knit polyester with raised pile; lightweight warmth | Hoodies, midlayers, outerwear |
| **Sherpa fleece** | Chunky looped pile; mimics shearling | Liners, coats |
| **Bonded fleece** | Two fabrics bonded back-to-back for structure | Outerwear panels |
| **Down (duck/goose)** | Natural feather fill; highest warmth-to-weight ratio | Puffer jackets |
| **PrimaLoft / synthetic fill** | Recycled polyester batting; retains warmth when wet | Puffer, sleeping bags |
| **Hollow-fibre fill** | Budget synthetic fill; less compressible than down | Affordable puffers |
| **Merino wool base layer** | Natural temperature regulation at 17–21 µm | Outdoor base layers |

**Fleece weights:**
- 100 weight: very lightweight; base layer use
- 200 weight: standard mid-layer (most common)
- 300 weight: heavyweight; standalone outerwear

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Polar fleece (anti-pilling 200 weight): USD 1.50–4.00/m. Sherpa fleece: USD 2.00–6.00/m. Synthetic fill (PrimaLoft equivalent): priced per weight of fill, USD 15–40/kg. Very widely available from Chinese manufacturers.

---

### 5.6 Antimicrobial & Odour-Resistant Fabrics

**Technologies:**
- **Silver ion treatment** (e.g., AgION, Sanitized): most effective; inhibits bacterial growth; durable to washing
- **Zinc pyrithione:** antifungal; used in socks and footwear
- **Triclosan:** largely being phased out due to regulatory concerns
- **Chitosan:** natural biopolymer from crustacean shells; growing use
- **Natural fibre antimicrobials:** Merino wool and bamboo have inherent (though mild) antimicrobial properties

**Garment applications:** Activewear, base layers, socks, outdoor wear, medical adjacent

**Sourcing/pricing signals:** Antimicrobial finish adds USD 0.20–0.80/m premium. Widely available. Request Oeko-TEX or third-party test verification.

## 6. Fabric Finishes & Treatments

Finishes are applied after fabric construction to change the surface appearance, texture, or performance. Many are applied in the Guangdong finishing mills adjacent to weaving and knitting clusters.

---

### 6.1 Washed Finishes

Used to soften fabric, create vintage character, or remove excess dye.

| Finish | Process | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Garment wash** | Whole garment washed post-sewing | Relaxed, worn-in look and feel |
| **Stone wash** | Garment washed with pumice stones | Distressed surface; softened denim |
| **Enzyme wash** | Bio-enzymes break down surface fibres | Smooth, soft, slightly abraded; eco-friendlier than stone |
| **Bleach wash** | Chlorine or ozone bleach treatment | Lightened, distressed colour |
| **Acid wash** | Chlorinated pumice stones | High-contrast marbled distress; denim classic |
| **Sand wash** | Sand particles in wash drum | Ultra-soft surface; slight sheen; popular in silk and satin |

**Canton Fair relevance:** Garment-finishing houses in Guangdong offer all these as standard services — buyers can request specific wash effects.

---

### 6.2 Brushed Finish

**Process:** Wire-covered rollers or emery paper physically raise surface fibres into a short, soft nap.

**Effect:** Flannel-like softness; slight colour-muting from raised surface fibres.

**Used on:** Cotton shirting (brushed cotton / flannel), fleece backing, peach-skin fabrics.

**Variants:**
- **Single-sided brush:** One face raised (standard flannel)
- **Double-sided brush:** Both faces raised (heavy flannel, Sherpa-back)
- **Raising / napping:** More aggressive version for heavier pile

---

### 6.3 Peached / Peach Skin Finish

**Process:** Fabric surface subjected to emery wheels or abrasive rollers; followed by enzyme treatment (cellulase) to remove excess loose fibres.

**Effect:** Ultra-soft, velvety, suede-like surface — similar to the skin of a ripe peach. Slightly matte appearance. Very popular in women's casualwear and sportswear.

**Used on:** Woven poplin, microfibre polyester, cotton blends.

**Note:** Distinguished from brushed by the smoothness of result — peaching is finer than standard brushing.

---

### 6.4 Enzyme Wash / Bio-Polishing

**Process:** Cellulase enzymes break down protruding fibre ends on the fabric surface.

**Key effects:**
- Removes pilling tendency permanently
- Smooth, clean surface appearance
- Colour clarity improved (brighter, cleaner dye depth)
- Softened hand feel without mechanical distortion

**Used on:** Cotton knitwear and wovens; denim; any cellulosic fabric.

**Sustainability note:** Enzyme treatments replace harsher chemical processes — generally considered more eco-friendly. Biodegradable process chemicals.

---

### 6.5 Coated & Laminated Finishes

| Finish | Description | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **PU coating** | Polyurethane applied to back of fabric | Water resistance, windproofing, structure |
| **PVC coating** | Vinyl applied to face or back | Waterproof; shiny; less breathable |
| **DWR (C0/PFC-free)** | Fluorine-free repellent treatment | Water beading without membrane; breathable |
| **Teflon (PTFE) coating** | Stain-repellent surface treatment | Stain resistance on face fabrics |
| **Silicone coating** | Soft-touch, pliable, water-repellent | Down fabric shells; lightweight outerwear |
| **Metallic coating** | Foil bonded to fabric surface | Fashion/trend applications; reflective safety |
| **Bonding** | Two fabrics fused with adhesive or heat | Structure, warmth, cleaner finish (no lining seams) |

---

### 6.6 Printed Finishes

| Method | Description | MOQ | Strengths |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Screen printing** | Colour pushed through mesh screens; one colour per screen | High (typically 1,000 m+) | Vibrant; cost-effective at volume |
| **Digital printing (inkjet)** | Inkjet direct-to-fabric; any design | Low (1–100 m possible) | Complex designs; photo-realistic; no MOQ barrier for design |
| **Transfer printing** | Design printed on transfer paper, heat-pressed to fabric | Medium | Works on synthetics; good colour range |
| **Sublimation** | Dye converted to gas, penetrates fibre (polyester only) | Medium (500+ m typical) | Permanent; wash-fast; vibrant on white/pale base |
| **Discharge printing** | Bleach removes base colour in pattern areas | High | Soft hand; no raised ink feel |
| **Burn-out (dévoré)** | Chemical destroys one fibre in blend, leaving pattern | Medium | Velvet-on-sheer effect; luxury aesthetic |
| **Foil printing** | Metallic foil heat-bonded in pattern | Low–medium | Fashion accent; metallic effects |

**Canton Fair relevance:** Digital printing suppliers at Canton Fair have dramatically lowered barriers — small batches and custom designs are increasingly viable. Sublimation is standard for polyester activewear. Screen print remains dominant for volume.

---

### 6.7 Other Common Finishes

| Finish | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Mercerising** | Cotton treated with NaOH; improves lustre, strength, dye uptake |
| **Sanforising** | Pre-shrinkage treatment; garment retains size after washing |
| **Singeing** | Fabric passed over flame; removes loose fibre hairs; smooth surface |
| **Calendering** | Pressure rollers; increases smoothness and sheen |
| **Anti-pilling** | Chemical or enzymatic; reduces surface pilling |
| **Wrinkle-resist / easy-care** | Resin finish; shirts maintain appearance after washing |
| **Flame retardant (FR)** | Chemical treatment; for safety workwear and children's sleepwear |
| **Soil release** | Finish that helps stains wash out more easily |

## 7. Canton Fair Context

### 7.5 Practical Sourcing Tips for Canton Fair

1. **Bring fabric swatches** from reference garments — compare against samples in-hall for hand feel, weight, and colour fidelity.
2. **Request test reports** for key specs: colourfastness (ISO 105), formaldehyde content (OEKO-TEX), shrinkage rate (ISO 6330), GSM (ISO 3801).
3. **Verify certifications at source:** GOTS at gots.net; GRS at control-union.com; TENCEL at Lenzing's brand portal.
4. **Digital printing suppliers** offer the lowest-MOQ path to custom designs — ideal for early-stage brands.
5. **Follow up within 2 weeks** of the fair — production slots fill fast and prices can shift.
6. **Negotiate holistically:** Unit price, MOQ, packaging, lead time, and shipping terms (FOB vs. CIF) are all negotiable.
7. **Start with stock fabrics** at low MOQ for sampling; move to custom constructions once a style is confirmed.
